## Terms, one name each, defined once at first use
- layer, sublayer (the attention or the MLP of a layer), F = a sublayer's output.
- running sum: Pre-LN updates h <- h + F(RN(h)), where RN is RMSNorm. The readout reads the sum.
- path: a way for an earlier output to reach a later layer without passing through the layers in between. Pre-LN has one path, the running sum.
- stream: Hyper-Connections (HC) keeps m copies of the state, m = 4 here. Each sublayer reads a weighted sum of the streams, writes F into each stream with its own weight, and mixes the streams with a matrix A. The head reads the sum of the streams.
- mHC: HC whose A is forced to be doubly stochastic by Sinkhorn iterations. Non-negative, rows and columns sum to 1, so the sum of the streams is unchanged.
- Attention Residuals (AttnRes): each sublayer reads a softmax-weighted sum of every earlier sublayer's output, with weights from a learned query and the current token's keys.
- routing: the weights that decide what goes where. In this post it has two parts that we remove separately: the mixing A, and the dependence of the weights on the token.
- ordinary model: trained on the last layer's next-token loss only. All runs here are ordinary models. Do not mention probes or separate heads in the text, except once in Open questions, with the sentence given below.
- held-out loss: next-token cross-entropy of the last layer on Paloma C4 text, in nats. Say "held-out loss" everywhere. Never "eval".

3. "Residual designs add paths and route between them." Define the terms above with the schematic [FIG-SCHEMATIC] (I draw it). Short. Use the running example, HC's learned mixing at layer 12: its four row sums are 0.81, 0.10, -0.47 and 0.86 in the MLP sublayer. A row sum tells how much of a stream goes on into the sum of the streams. One is negative, so that stream is subtracted.
4. Setup. At most three short sentences or one short paragraph: 48 layers, width 512, MLP 1792, 2.6B tokens of FineWeb-Edu, the MuonH recipe of the Marin speedrun, every variant trained from scratch for the same 4,959 steps with the same data order. Held-out text is Paloma C4.
5. "Removing the routing does not raise the loss." [FIG-REMOVAL]. HC without mixing: lower by 0.03 to 0.08 nats. mHC without mixing: higher by 0.006. AttnRes with weights that ignore the token: lower by 0.08 to 0.18. HC with constant mixing, also ignoring the token: lower by 0.006 to 0.047. The largest rise from any removal is 0.015 nats (mHC with linear read and write). These differences are measured between full trainings. Return to the running example: without the mixing, nothing is subtracted at layer 12, and the model is better.
6. "The controls do not explain it." Three controls, in plain prose. (a) Parameter counts are the same within 0.2%: use params_M in facts.json. (b) Same runs on the earlier attention code differ by about 0.01: Pre-LN 3.668 and 3.674. (c) AttnRes's loss spikes explain only part of it: with its sources scaled to unit RMS it has no spikes and reaches 3.611, still above 3.562. (d) A strong objection: is the mixing simply unused? No. On real tokens mHC's mixing moves about 3 percent per sublayer, and replacing it with the identity after training raises the loss from 2.873 to 3.480 (FineWeb-Edu tokens, a different text from the held-out numbers; say so). A model trained with mixing relies on it. A model trained without it does as well. So the removal has to be done before training.
7. "Training failed only in models with routing." [FIG-FAILURES]. AttnRes: the last layer's training loss jumps by up to 10.4 nats above its running median, 5 and 10 times in the two trainings. With input-independent weights, no such jump. HC: one training ended at a held-out loss of 9.885. From step 4,408 the state norm passes the point where a float32 sum of squares overflows, about 1.8e19; RMSNorm then returns zero, so the readout gives a uniform prediction, for 70 percent of the tokens at the end, and the gradient norm stays flat, so gradient monitors see nothing. RMSNorm reads only the direction, so nothing in the loss limits the size of the state, and HC's row sums above 1 let it grow. HC without mixing reached a median state norm of 667 at the last layer. State this mechanism as measured for this run; do not generalize.
8. "The gain comes from the paths." [FIG-PATHS]. One path: 3.660 to 3.691. Several paths with no mixing: 3.562 to 3.582, about 0.09 to 0.11 nats lower. HC with one stream has learned gains and writes and no second path: 3.675, the same as Pre-LN. So learned scales alone do not pay. With mixing kept, going from 1 to 2 streams lowers the loss from 3.675 to 3.632, and 4 or 8 streams add nothing clear (3.615 to 3.656, 3.643).
9. Limitations (three or four sentences). One size (48 layers, width 512, 2.6B tokens), one optimizer (MuonH), one held-out text; larger models such as Kimi K3 are not tested and may differ; we removed the parts of the routing one at a time, and we did not train a model with all of them gone in HC. HC without mixing keeps its token-dependent read and write. 10. Takeaways. To sum up in two or three sentences. Practical advice marked as advice: if you want extra paths, start with a few streams and identity mixing, or with fixed weights over earlier layers, and add learned routing only if it beats that baseline on your scale. Watch the state norm, since the loss does not show its growth until it overflows.
