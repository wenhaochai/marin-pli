# Residual-connection designs that mix information across depth: sources, internals, static vs dynamic routing

Scope note: every arXiv id below was opened (abs page, HTML or PDF text) in this session unless it is marked "(via reference list)" or "(snippet only)". Full texts of AttnRes, HC, mHC, DenseFormer, MUDDFormer, DCA, VRL, LAuReL, Curse of Depth (LNS), KEEL, MoDA and DepthBench were downloaded and read from `/scratch/gpfs/GROUP/USER/tmp/litpdfs/*.txt`. Percent-of-gain numbers marked "(my arithmetic)" are computed by me from the papers' tables.

## Q1. Primary sources, exact titles and key claims

### Takeaway
Every design in DepthBench exists and has a citable primary source. Only Hyper-Connections (HC) and Full AttnRes keep improving as models get deeper and narrower in the one controlled cross-architecture benchmark (DepthBench). The norm and scale variants (LNS, DeepNorm, KEEL, Sandwich-LN) do not. Static-weight dense connections (DenseFormer) give small or no gains in other groups' harnesses.

### Cited Findings

**Multi-stream (widened residual) family**
- Hyper-Connections. Defa Zhu, Hongzhi Huang, Zihao Huang, Yutao Zeng, Yunyao Mao, Banggu Wu, Qiyang Min, Xun Zhou (ByteDance). arXiv:2409.19606 (v1 29 Sep 2024, v3 18 Mar 2025). Published at ICLR 2025 (the PDF footer says so). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606)
  - n streams with a learnable (n+1)×(n+1) matrix: depth weights B (write), width weights A_m (read) and A_r (stream mixing). Static HC (SHC) uses learned scalars. Dynamic HC (DHC) adds `s·tanh(norm(H)·W)` to each static term, with W initialized to zero. The static init makes the net equal to Pre-Norm: B = ones, A_m = e_(k mod n), A_r = I. Output weights are scaled by √n at init. — [HC HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
  - OLMo-1B, 500B tokens, V2 eval loss: baseline 2.811, DHC×1 2.819 (worse than baseline), DHC×2 2.802, DHC×4 2.781, DHC×8 2.778. — [HC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
  - OLMo-7B: V2 loss 2.581 → 2.559. Task average 70.1 → 71.0. The baseline has frequent loss spikes; DHC×4 has none. OLMoE-1B-7B: ARC-C 41.8 → 47.8. — [HC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
- mHC: Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections. Zhenda Xie, Yixuan Wei, Huanqi Cao et al., DeepSeek-AI. arXiv:2512.24880 (v2 5 Jan 2026). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880)
  - H_res is projected to approximately doubly stochastic matrices by Sinkhorn–Knopp (20 iterations). H_pre = σ(·) and H_post = 2σ(·). Gating scalars are initialized to 0.01. n = 4. — [mHC v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24880v2)
  - HC's composite-mapping gain reaches peaks of about 3000 and the loss surges near step 12k (27B). mHC cuts the composite gain to about 1.6. Overhead is 6.7% at n = 4. At 27B mHC lowers final loss by 0.021 vs the baseline. BBH: 43.8 baseline / 48.9 HC / 51.0 mHC. MATH: 22.0 / 26.4 / 26.0. — [mHC](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24880v2)
  - The paper says H_res "yields the most significant performance gain" among the three maps. — [mHC](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24880v2)
- mHC is one of three named upgrades in DeepSeek-V4 ("DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence", arXiv:2606.19348, 26 Apr 2026). V4-Pro has 1.6T total / 49B active parameters, V4-Flash 284B / 13B. Both are trained with Muon on more than 32T tokens. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348)
- DepthBench notes that "later HC/mHC implementations, such as DeepSeek V4, may instead use a learnable final mixer" in place of the sum of streams. I have not confirmed this in the V4 text. — [DepthBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- Frac-Connections: Fractional Extension of Hyper-Connections. Zhu, Huang, Zhou et al. arXiv:2503.14125 (2025). It splits the hidden state into fractions instead of copying it. Largest run: a 7B MoE on up to 3T tokens. (snippet only) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14125)
- mHC-lite: You Don't Need 20 Sinkhorn-Knopp Iterations. Yongyi Yang, Jianyang Gao. arXiv:2601.05732 (via reference list). — [AttnRes ref 64](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05732)
- Tian Xie, "Your DeepSeek mHC Might Not Need the 'm'" (Zhihu, 2026). Both the AttnRes report and Su Jianlin cite it for the claim that setting H_res = I "still yields competitive performance" (via reference list; I did not open the post). — [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2010852389670908320); [AttnRes §6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031); [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)

**Cross-layer access (dense or attention over depth) family**
- Attention Residuals. Kimi Team (Guangyu Chen*, Yu Zhang*, Jianlin Su* et al.), arXiv:2603.15031 (16 Mar 2026). Code: github.com/MoonshotAI/Attention-Residuals. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15031)
  - Full AttnRes: h_l = Σ_i α_{i→l} v_i with α ∝ exp(w_lᵀ RMSNorm(v_i)). v_0 is the embedding and v_i = f_i(h_i) for each attention or MLP sublayer. The query w_l is a static learned vector. The query must be zero-initialized, so training starts from a uniform average. — [AttnRes PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - Block AttnRes: the sublayer outputs inside a block are summed. Attention runs over N block sums, the embedding and the current partial sum. N ≈ 8 "recovers most of the benefit". — [AttnRes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - Scaling fits: baseline 1.891·C^−0.057, Block 1.870·C^−0.058, Full 1.865·C^−0.057. At 5.6 PFLOP/s-days Block AttnRes reaches 1.692 vs 1.714 for the baseline, a 1.25× compute advantage. — [AttnRes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - Kimi Linear 48B total / 3B active parameters, 27 blocks (54 layers), Block AttnRes with 6 layers per block (9 blocks plus the embedding = 10 sources). Trained with Muon on 1T tokens plus ≈400B of mid-training. GPQA-Diamond 36.9 → 44.4, Math 53.5 → 57.1, HumanEval 59.1 → 62.2, MMLU 73.5 → 74.6. — [AttnRes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - Under a fixed budget (6.5e19 FLOPs, 2.3e8 active parameters) the optimum moves from d_model/L_b ≈ 60 (baseline, 1.847) to ≈ 45 (AttnRes, 1.802). AttnRes therefore favors deeper models. — [AttnRes Fig. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence. Kimi Team (Tongtong Bai and 400 others). arXiv:2607.24653 (v1 27 Jul 2026, v2 7 Aug 2026). 2.8T total / 104B active parameters. Built on Kimi Delta Attention and Attention Residuals, with 16 of 896 routed experts. The authors report about 2.5× scaling efficiency over K2. The abstract gives no layer count, so the 93-layer figure in the project notes is not confirmed here. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)
  - Su Jianlin: "K3 = KDA + MLA + Stable LatentMoE + AttnRes". The optimizer is the Moonlight version of Muon, with attention weights optimized per head. — [kexue 11848](https://kexue.fm/archives/11848)
  - DepthBench says it follows "original AttnRes and Kimi K3 settings to fix the number of boundaries at 8", which implies K3 uses about 8 blocks. — [DepthBench appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- DenseFormer: Enhancing Information Flow in Transformers via Depth Weighted Averaging. Matteo Pagliardini, Amirkeivan Mohtashami, François Fleuret, Martin Jaggi. arXiv:2402.02622 (2024). A Depth-Weighted-Average (DWA) step after each block, with static scalar weights. The DWA is initialized to the identity (α_{i,i} = 1, the rest 0), which is exactly the standard Transformer. It reaches the perplexity of much deeper Transformers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)
- MUDDFormer: Breaking Residual Bottlenecks in Transformers via Multiway Dynamic Dense Connections. Da Xiao, Qingye Meng, Shengping Li, Xingyuan Yuan. arXiv:2502.12170, ICML 2025. Connection weights come from an MLP of the hidden state at each position, separately for the Q, K, V and residual inputs. The authors report matching a Transformer trained with ~1.8–2.4× the compute. MUDDPythia-2.8B matches Pythia-6.9B, for +0.23% parameters and +0.4% compute. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170)
- DeepCrossAttention: Supercharging Transformer Residual Connections. Mike Heddes, Adel Javanmard, Kyriakos Axiotis, Gang Fu, MohammadHossein Bateni, Vahab Mirrokni. arXiv:2502.06785, ICML 2025. GRN-v1 (static scalar weights = DenseFormer), GRN-v2 (static per-dimension weights), GRN-v3 (adds an input-dependent term). DCA = 3 GRN-v3 instances per block for Q, K and V. The authors report reaching the Transformer's quality "up to 3x faster". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06785)
- MoDA: Mixture-of-Depths Attention. Lianghui Zhu, Yuxin Fang, Bencheng Liao, …, Xinggang Wang (HUST and ByteDance Seed). arXiv:2603.15619 (16 Mar 2026). Each attention head attends over the sequence KV of the current layer plus the same-token "depth KV" of earlier layers. At 1.5B: −0.2 average perplexity on 10 validation sets and +2.11% on 10 downstream tasks, for 3.7% extra FLOPs. The kernel reaches 97.3% of FlashAttention-2 efficiency at 64K. The authors find MoDA works better with post-norm than with pre-norm. Code: github.com/hustvl/MoDA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15619)
- Value Residual Learning (ResFormer). Zhanchao Zhou, Tianyi Wu, Zhiyun Jiang, Fares Obeid, Zhenzhong Lan. arXiv:2410.17897, ACL 2025. V_n = λ_{n,1}V_1 + λ_{n,2}H_{n−1}W_n^V, with constant, sparse or learnable λ. It reaches the same validation loss with 16.11% fewer parameters and 20.3% less data. The hand-tuned Sparse-ResFormer and Learnable-ResFormer-Plus beat plain Learnable-ResFormer. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)
- LAuReL: Learned Augmented Residual Layer. Gaurav Menghani, Ravi Kumar, Sanjiv Kumar. arXiv:2411.07501, ICML 2025. Variants: RW (x_{i+1} = αf(x_i) + βx_i, normalized with softmax or sigmoid), LR (low-rank BA added to the identity) and PA (k previous activations). On 1B and 4B LLMs it reports +2.54% to +20.05% on downstream tasks for +0.012% and +0.1% parameters. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07501)
- Further cross-layer works named in the AttnRes report (via reference list, not opened): MRLA arXiv:2302.03985; ANCRe arXiv:2602.09009; Dreamer (Depth-Recurrent Attention Mixtures) arXiv:2601.21582; DDL (Deep Delta Learning) arXiv:2601.00417; SiameseNorm arXiv:2602.08064; Residual Matrix Transformers arXiv:2506.22696; HybridNorm arXiv:2503.04598. Su Jianlin adds SKNets, LIMe (arXiv:2502.09245) and DCA as works the report missed. — [AttnRes refs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031); [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)

### Inferences
- The field has two architectural ideas that keep paying off at depth: state expansion (HC) and selective direct access (Full AttnRes). Scaling a single stream (LNS, DeepNorm, KEEL) does not, at least under AdamW at 400M (DepthBench). KEEL's own paper disagrees at 64–1024 layers with its own recipe.
- Production adoption is split: DeepSeek-V4 uses mHC, and Kimi K3 uses Block AttnRes with about 8 blocks. Both labs train with Muon. That makes Muon-family optimizers the relevant regime for the project's MuonH runs.

## Q2. Static vs input-dependent (dynamic) depth mixing: direct comparisons

### Takeaway
Six papers compare static and dynamic mixing in a matched setting. Static mixing keeps roughly 0% to 100% of the dynamic gain, with most estimates at 40–70% (my arithmetic). The result depends strongly on two things. (a) Whether the static scheme can represent the residual sum as a special case and starts from it. (b) Whether selection is normalized (softmax over RMS-normed keys). AttnRes is itself "semi-static": its query is a static vector, and the only input dependence comes from the keys (the layer outputs).

### Cited Findings
- **Hyper-Connections (OLMo-1B, 500B tokens; V2 / V3 eval loss).** Baseline 2.811 / 2.544. SHC×2 2.799 / 2.538 vs DHC×2 2.802 / 2.534: no difference at n = 2. SHC×4 2.791 / 2.528 vs DHC×4 2.781 / 2.515: "DHC notably better at n = 4". — [HC Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
  - (my arithmetic) At n = 4, SHC keeps 0.020/0.030 = 67% (V2) and 0.016/0.029 = 55% (V3) of DHC's gain over the baseline. At n = 2 it keeps ≥100%.
  - Vision (ImageNet ViT/16): Base SHC 77.60 vs DHC 77.26; Large SHC 78.38 vs DHC 79.94. — [HC appendix E.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
  - Freezing the width connections WC at init (no tanh) gives V2 2.804, nearly the baseline. Freezing B gives 2.781 vs 2.779 with both trained. The static stream-mixing and read maps carry the benefit; the write vector B matters less. — [HC Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
- **AttnRes (16-layer, 436M-active MoE row of the scaling table; validation loss).** Baseline 1.766. Full AttnRes (static query, dynamic keys) 1.737. "w/ input-dependent query" (query projected from the hidden state) 1.731. "w/ input-independent mixing" (query and key replaced by learned scalars) 1.749. DenseFormer 1.767. mHC 1.747. Sigmoid instead of softmax 1.741. No RMSNorm on keys 1.743 (Full) and 1.750 (Block). Multi-head depth attention (H = 16) on Block 1.752 vs 1.746. Sliding window (W = 1 + 8) 1.764. — [AttnRes Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - (my arithmetic) Fully static mixing keeps 0.017/0.029 = 59% of Full AttnRes's gain and 49% of the input-dependent-query gain. DenseFormer keeps 0%.
  - The authors write that DenseFormer "shows no gain over the baseline (1.767), highlighting the importance of input-dependent weighting." — [AttnRes §5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
  - Su Jianlin chose the static query "for convenience" in the first prototype, and that version was already "very significant". More complex designs were mostly worse; only RMSNorm on K gave a stable gain. The static query is what lets all of a block's attention weights be computed ahead of time (the "two-phase" inference). — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)
- **MUDDFormer (405M, Pile perplexity).** Transformer++ 11.68. +static dense 11.44. +dynamic dense 11.09. +multiway static dense 11.27. +multiway dynamic dense 10.83. Full MUDDFormer 10.77. "All three ingredients … static, dynamic and multiway, make contributions." Removing dense connections from the value stream hurts most (11.05), then the residual stream (11.14). — [MUDDFormer Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170)
  - (my arithmetic) Static keeps 0.24/0.59 = 41% of the dynamic gain (single-way) and 0.41/0.85 = 48% (multiway).
  - On scaling curves, both DenseFormer and HC show a "clear advantage over Transformer++", and DDFormer (dynamic) beats both. — [MUDDFormer §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170)
- **DeepCrossAttention (6-layer, LM1B perplexity).** Transformer 18.98. GRN-v1 (static scalar = DenseFormer) 18.80. GRN-v2 (static per-dimension) 18.43. GRN-v3 (+input-dependent term) 18.41. DCA (three GRN-v3 per block, for Q, K and V) 18.06. "The biggest improvement in perplexity comes from GRN-v1, followed by DCA and GRN-v2." — [DCA Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06785)
  - (my arithmetic) Static per-dimension weights keep 0.55/0.57 = 96% of the single-instance dynamic gain. The large extra gain comes from multiway (separate Q/K/V mixtures), not from input dependence. This matches MUDDFormer.
  - The DCA gain shrinks with width (Δ perplexity −2.82 at width 64, −0.39 at 1024) but holds with depth. — [DCA Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06785)
  - LM1B 6-layer comparison: LAuReL-PA 18.99, 1×1-DenseFormer 18.80, HC 18.65, DCA 18.06. — [DCA Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06785)
- **mHC for parameter-efficient fine-tuning** (frozen OLMo-2-1B, Tulu-3, n = 4, 3k steps). Test loss: no adapter 2.00. Static mHC 1.79, static mHC-lite 1.77, static KromHC 1.77. Dynamic versions: 1.39, 1.40, 1.35. Static uses 384–1,024 parameters and dynamic uses 3.4M–8.7M, so the comparison confounds dynamics with adapter capacity. — [arXiv:2607.18130](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18130)
- **Value residual.** Learnable λ does not beat hand-set sparse constant λ (Sparse-ResFormer, Learnable-ResFormer-Plus). — [VRL §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)
- DepthBench labels HC, mHC, AttnRes and MoDA "dynamic" and the norm variants "fixed". It "does not isolate static versus dynamic effects experimentally". — [DepthBench Table 1, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)

### Inferences
- Across papers, the static part of a learned depth router carries 40–100% of the gain. Dynamic gains grow with expansion rate (HC n = 4 vs n = 2) and with selectivity (softmax + RMSNorm keys). The project's own freeze test (78–95% of the reset cost avoided) sits at the high end. It measures something weaker than a from-scratch static model, because the dynamically trained weights have already co-adapted. I expect from-scratch static runs to keep less than the freeze test suggests.
- **Why DenseFormer gets 0% in the AttnRes harness but a positive gain elsewhere.** DenseFormer adds a DWA after the residual sum, with unnormalized signed weights and identity init. AttnRes replaces the residual with a softmax over RMS-normed keys, starting from uniform averaging. Su notes that with an input RMSNorm, uniform averaging ≡ equal-weight sum (RMSNorm(cx) = RMSNorm(x)). — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664) So zero-query AttnRes computes exactly the Pre-LN function at init. Any static design that starts at and contains "residual sum" is a fair baseline; one that cannot represent it (sliding window) loses. Su says SWA "was even worse than Residuals". The paper's Table 4 has SWA at 1.764 vs 1.766, which conflicts slightly with the blog. — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664); [AttnRes Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- **Prediction for the queued "HC with static routing from scratch".** It should land between SHC×4 (≈55–67% of the DHC gain, OLMo-1B, 500B tokens) and the freeze test (78–95%). The run is short (2.6B tokens) and DHC's dynamic gains start at 0.01, so the dynamic terms have little time to grow. I expect the static share at the high end, ≈70–100% of HC's gain over Pre-LN. The one risk: HC's dynamic tanh terms may act as a stabilizer. DepthBench saw HC gradient explosion at the deepest 500M shape. Under MuonH with fixed weight norms, an explosion would have to come from the routing scalars themselves (Adam-trained, no weight decay).
- **Prediction for the queued "Full AttnRes with static routing from scratch"** (one learned softmax logit per (reader, source)). The Kimi ablation (59% kept at 16 layers) is the closest match. At 48 layers, with a 53,000× source in the dynamic model, I expect less to survive (≈30–60%), for this reason. A static weight cannot exclude a huge-norm source only for some tokens. Its α·v term enters every token's mixture unless α ≲ 1e-5, which needs a logit gap of ≳11.5 nats. So either the static model never grows such sources, or it must assign near-zero weight to them for every reader except one. A check that tests this: measure the max/median source-norm ratio in the static model. Prediction: no source above ~10²× the median.
- The very large "uniform weights" penalty in the ordinary AttnRes models (7.5–13.5 nats) fits the identity above. Resetting to uniform turns the model into a Pre-LN network that runs AttnRes-trained weights it was never trained to work with as Pre-LN. The cost measures co-adaptation more than the value of mixing.

### Gaps
- No paper trains static vs dynamic routing at ≥48 layers, and none uses Muon or norm-constrained (MuonH-style) optimizers in the comparison. Kimi's ablation is 16 layers; HC's is OLMo-1B (16 layers).
- The AttnRes "input-independent mixing" row does not say whether the scalars pass through softmax or how they are initialized. I could not resolve this from the text.

## Q3. Learned mixing weights at convergence: locality, embedding reliance, sparsity, block ends, massive activations

### Takeaway
Every learned depth router converges to a similar picture. (1) A dominant diagonal (the immediate predecessor). (2) Persistent, large weight on the token embedding, often signed: positive early, negative late. (3) A few long-range "skip" sources. (4) A late "aggregation" block that mixes many sources before the output. Softmax routers become sharper (more decisive) under block compression. Depth-softmax normalization without a "null" option goes with heavier sinks and activation outliers. In Block AttnRes, layers before the first saved block put essentially zero weight on their own running sum.

### Cited Findings
- **AttnRes weights (16-layer model, token-averaged).** "Each layer attends most strongly to its immediate predecessor, yet selective off-diagonal concentrations emerge (e.g., layer 4 attending to early sources, layers 15–16 reaching back under the block setting)." "The embedding h1 retains non-trivial weight throughout, especially in pre-attention layers." "Pre-MLP inputs show sharper diagonal reliance on recent representations, while pre-attention inputs maintain broader receptive fields." "Block attention (N = 8) recovers the essential structure with sharper, more decisive weight distributions." The authors also name "depth-wise attention sinks … certain layers consistently attract high weight regardless of input". — [AttnRes Fig. 8, §5.4.2, §6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- Su: the embedding is its own block in Block AttnRes because the Full-AttnRes attention maps showed "considerable attention to the embedding layer". — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)
- **Block AttnRes dead segment (DepthBench).** "Before the first block output has been saved, the depth-mixing softmax assigns essentially zero weight to the running sum: each layer's own computation is dropped from the mixed input, and the network effectively keeps re-reading the embedding." The dead segment is 2, 5 and 8 layers at L = 16, 24, 32, "so a quarter of the L = 32 network can not benefit from stacking layers". The authors propose this as why Block AttnRes stops gaining from depth. — [DepthBench §4.3, Fig. 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- **Block AttnRes magnitudes.** In the 48B model, Block AttnRes "confines this growth within each block, as selective aggregation at block boundaries resets the accumulation, yielding a bounded periodic pattern" of output magnitude. The baseline grows monotonically and has the largest gradients in the earliest layers. — [AttnRes Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- **DenseFormer weights (48 and 72 blocks).** The pattern is stable across depths. "High weights are on the diagonal … as well as on the immediate previous blocks." "High weights are given to the initial embedding vectors. Those weights are positive in earlier layers while later layers assign a negative weight." "An aggregation block is observed near the final layers where a high weight is given to all previous layers in the block." — [DenseFormer §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)
  - Small weights matter: "dropping beyond 15% of the weights leads to a significant increase in perplexity". "Last K" (K = 4) sparsity reaches 18.28 and "Connect to Last" 18.33, vs 17.96 / 17.87 for dilated DenseFormers and a 18.61 baseline. — [DenseFormer Fig. 7, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)
  - Cosine similarity to the input embedding stays high early and drops in late layers. The authors read this as the model removing current-token information before predicting the next token. — [DenseFormer Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)
- **HC weights.** A Λ-shaped pattern: layers rely on a few adjacent layers (a Post-Norm-like decay), and bottom layers (e.g. 0 and 2) are reused often (Pre-Norm-like). The input embedding feeds most layers but not the final one. There are parallel-like blocks (layer 11 contributes little to layer 12). Attention layers, especially at the bottom, have few long-range connections, and FFN outputs are much larger. In OLMo-1B-DHC×1, "layer 17 is wasted, who has no connection to subsequent layers at all." — [HC §4.5, App. F](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
- **HC and AttnRes under DepthBench probes (L = 32).** Token-mean HC path weights w_{i→ℓ} = β_iᵀA_{i+1}…A_{ℓ−1}α_ℓ are "strongest locally but remain substantial over longer ranges". Layer pruning on ARC-Easy: Pre-LN and HC are "dominated by the first layer"; Full AttnRes depends strongly on both its first and final layers. Logit lens (HC read out as the sum of 4 streams): Pre-LN approaches the final prediction monotonically. HC is also smooth "but its earlier representations remain farther from the final prediction". Full AttnRes is "markedly less monotonic, consistent with continued retrieval and late integration". Pairs with causal score >0.45 in the last three quarters: Pre-LN ~1%, HC ~7%, Full AttnRes ~16%. — [DepthBench §4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- **mHC transport collapse.** The effective rank of composed residual products is 1.44–1.65 for mHC vs 2.53–2.81 for HC. At L = 32, mHC's propagated stream directions become nearly collinear. The mechanism: doubly stochastic maps share U = (1/m)11ᵀ as a fixed point, so long products tend to U. — [DepthBench §4.3, Fig. 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- **Sinks and outliers in AttnRes (OASIS).** "Attention Sinks and Outliers in Attention Residuals", Haozheng Luo et al., arXiv:2605.17887 (v2 24 Sep 2026). In LLaMA-3.2-1B with AttnRes, the ⟨|begin_of_text|⟩ sink "becomes substantially more severe". Kurtosis exceeds 100 in the first layer and stays at 50–70, vs ≈3 for vanilla. The proposed cause: "standard Softmax allocates all probability mass to active tokens and depth branches, leaving no null pathway". The fix is Softmax₁ (a null slot) at both the token and depth levels. Reported results: max ‖x‖∞ −81.75% and W8A8 perplexity −82%. The page has internal inconsistencies (‖x‖∞ ≈28 in the text vs 84.43 in a table). — [arXiv:2605.17887](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17887)
- "A Unified View of Attention and Residual Sinks" (arXiv:2601.22966) defines "residual sinks" as fixed dimensions with large activations across tokens, linked to massive activations. Example: Qwen3-235B-A22B dimensions >1000 on the first token. (snippet only) — [arXiv:2601.22966](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22966v1)
- arXiv:2609.08574 ("Do New Attention Mechanisms Actually Fix Attention Sinks at Million-Token Context?") reportedly finds that Block AttnRes lowers the largest activation from 97.9 to 38.9 in a hybrid stack, while sink mass does not fall. (snippet only) — [arXiv:2609.08574](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08574)
- **MUDDFormer internals.** Cosine similarity between adjacent-layer inputs exceeds 0.97 in late Pythia layers; MUDDPythia stays lower, most of all in the value stream. The attention-head "activation ratio" (heads not parked on sinks) is ~2.4× higher across layers. — [MUDDFormer §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170)
- **Pre-LN depth inefficiency.** Pruning deep layers of Pre-LN LLMs costs little. Angular distance between consecutive deep layers falls below 0.2 (DeepSeek-7B after layer 18) and approaches zero over the last third of LLaMA2-13B. — [Curse of Depth §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05795)

### Inferences (mapped to project findings)
- **Near-binary reads in AttnRes block 1 (logit gaps 32–67).** This matches DepthBench's "dead segment", where the softmax puts ≈0 on the running sum before the first block is saved and re-reads the embedding. The project's Full-AttnRes finding is the general form: softmax selection over RMS-normed keys, with |w| free to grow (Adam, no weight decay on queries in the project). Saturation is therefore cheap. A logit gap of 32 needs only |w|·Δ(normalized key) ≈ 32, and the keys have RMS 1. In DepthBench the queries have weight decay; in the project they do not. The project should expect larger gaps than DepthBench's models.
- **The 53,000× source at layer 44 read only by the layer-48 MLP.** The same pattern appears in massive activations (Sun et al.: input-independent, bias-like, up to 1e5×) and in OASIS (AttnRes without a null route grows outliers). AttnRes normalizes keys but not values, so a huge-norm value is harmless only if every reader except one gives it α ≈ 0. With RMSNorm keys, the magnitude does not change its score, so the selection is magnitude-blind and the huge vector can exist safely. A testable account: the source works as a constant bias channel for the final MLP. That predicts (i) its direction is nearly token-independent (cosine to its mean >0.9), and (ii) replacing it by its token mean costs little.
- **K3 reads its current block; prediction forms in the last ~15 layers; block-end layers write large vectors the output avoids.** Both the AttnRes Fig. 8 diagonal dominance and DepthBench's late integration (non-monotone logit lens) predict the first two. Large block-end writes fit the "periodic bounded magnitude" pattern: the partial sum is reset at boundaries, so the last layer of a block must write a large update to steer its own block sum. The output (which reads block sums through softmax with RMSNorm keys) can then down-weight that block. I found no paper that documents block-end write magnitudes directly.
- **HC readouts sum streams, so sign-cancelling writes are invisible.** In the original HC implementation and in DepthBench, the final output is the sum of the 4 streams (mHC in DepthBench averages them). DeepSeek V4 may use a learned final mixer (per DepthBench). A write vector β with entries that sum to ≈0 is invisible to a sum readout but visible to later layers' read vectors α. This is the stream analogue of DenseFormer's signed embedding weights.
- **HC layer-1 stream mixing (≈0.39, 2.76, 2.84, −0.81) carries most of the value.** This matches HC's Λ pattern (bottom layers reused often), the DepthBench pruning result (HC dominated by the first layer) and DenseFormer's signed embedding weights (positive early, negative late). The negative entry (−0.81) gives a way to subtract the embedding later. AttnRes cannot do that, because its weights are non-negative by design. Su argues non-negativity makes learning "friendlier". — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)
- **HC reaches the head's coordinates by 3/4 depth.** This is in tension with DepthBench's logit lens, where HC's early layers stay farther from the final prediction than Pre-LN's. The two measurements differ (project: coordinate alignment to the head; DepthBench: KL of the decoded summed stream). If HC writes in sign-cancelling combinations, a summed-stream logit lens would hide early alignment that the head reads only at the end. This tension needs one shared probe.

### Gaps
- No paper reports static-vs-dynamic routing weights side by side at convergence, or how weight sharpness (logit gaps) changes with depth.
- I found no published per-source norm ratios for Full AttnRes (a 53,000×-type statistic). OASIS reports kurtosis and ‖x‖∞ only, in a retrofitted LLaMA setting.

## Q4. Parametrization: Pre-LN / Post-LN / DeepNorm / KEEL / LNS equivalences, and how the optimizer breaks them

### Takeaway
DeepNorm and KEEL's residual up-scaling is a reparametrization because RMSNorm/LN is scale invariant: LN(αx + F) = LN(x + F/α). LNS is Pre-LN with a fixed 1/√ℓ multiplier on the normalized input. These equivalences hold for the function only. With Adam or Muon the per-parameter update size does not follow the reparametrization, so the effective learning rate of each branch changes. DepthBench's tuned LRs show this: LNS needs 5× the LR (1e-2) and DeepNorm half (1e-3) of Pre-LN's 2e-3.

### Cited Findings
- Su Jianlin writes DeepNorm as LN(αx_l + F(x_l)) = LN(x_l + F(x_l)/α). His analysis of DeepNet's "increment explosion" is a scale argument about update size per step (ΔL = O(ηNK) under SGD with O(1) gradients). The analysis is optimizer-dependent by construction. — [kexue 8978](https://kexue.fm/archives/8978)
- Admin likewise puts Transformer instability in the residual branch's amplification of parameter updates. — [arXiv:2004.08249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08249)
- KEEL vs DeepNorm, in the authors' words. DeepNorm "relies heavily on a specialized initialization scheme (scaling weights by L^−0.25)", and as weights drift over long training "the benefits of a specific initialization strategy often diminish". KEEL "bak[es] the stabilization mechanism into the architecture itself". In their ablations, naive α = L scaling without the inner LN lagged Pre-LN. A learnable input scale β (init 1) caused "gradient attenuation … as β decreases". Adding LN before β fixed it. — [KEEL §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19895)
- DepthBench (AdamW, wd 0.1). LNS "introduces no parameters but requires a 5× larger optimal learning rate (1 × 10−2)". DeepNorm's optimum is 1e-3, the others' 2e-3. Pre-LN, Sandwich-LN, LNS, DeepNorm and KEEL "converge to similar deep representations" (angular distance). DepthBench makes no equivalence claim. — [DepthBench App. A, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- LNS paper: combining LNS with scaled init (GPT-2/OLMo-style output-projection down-scaling) "diminishes the effectiveness of LNS". — [LNS footnote 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05795)
- AttnRes report: Pre-LN hidden-state norm grows O(L), and later layers "learn increasingly larger outputs" to stay influential. Block AttnRes bounds this. — [AttnRes §2, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- Su Jianlin on norm-constrained training: options are fixing ‖W‖ by renormalizing each step (Hyperball; Nemotron-Flash arXiv:2511.18890), reparametrizing f(x; τω/‖ω‖), or steepest descent on the sphere. He argues for bounding ‖W‖ ≤ τ rather than fixing it, because fixing it "still breaks the original training dynamics". — [kexue 11729](https://kexue.fm/archives/11729); [Hyperball blog](https://whenwen.github.io/wd_blog/public/hyperball-part-1.html)
- With an input RMSNorm, AttnRes's weighted average equals a weighted sum (RMSNorm(cx) = RMSNorm(x)), so the Σα = 1 constraint costs no expressivity. — [kexue 11664](https://kexue.fm/archives/11664)

### Inferences (derivations; not from a paper)
- **LNS ≡ Pre-LN with RMSNorm gain g·(1/√ℓ).** Pre-LN with gain init 1/√ℓ computes the same function at init. The difference is in training. In LNS, Adam updates g at rate ~lr, so the effective gain c·g (c = 1/√ℓ) moves at c·lr. In the queued run, the effective gain moves at lr. The queued "Pre-LN with LNS initial gains" is therefore exactly LNS with the gain LR multiplied by √ℓ (up to √48 ≈ 6.9× in the last layer). Under MuonH the matrices keep their norms, so all other dynamics match. The run tests whether LNS's benefit is (a) an init effect, so the queued run ≈ LNS, or (b) a persistent per-layer brake on gain movement, so the queued run drifts toward Pre-LN as deep-layer gains grow back toward O(1). Read the deep-layer gain RMS over training; it separates (a) from (b) directly.
- **What the 1/√ℓ factor does is not just a 1/√ℓ output scale.** For attention, q and k both scale by c, so logits scale by c² = 1/ℓ: deep layers start with near-uniform attention. For SwiGLU, (cx)W_gate passes through SiLU, which is ≈ x/2 near 0, so the gated product scales ≈ c² in the small-input regime and ≈ c in the large-input regime. LNS therefore cools attention temperature and MLP gain with depth. Under MuonH with fixed weight norms, only the gains can undo this. That makes the gain LR (the thing the queued run changes) the deciding factor.
- **DeepNorm/KEEL under MuonH.** LN(αx + F(x)) = LN(x + F(x)/α). Down-weighting a branch by 1/α with a fixed-norm W_out is the same function as a W_out with norm 1/α. Under MuonH the norm cannot drift, so the 1/α is permanent rather than an init that fades, which removes KEEL's main complaint about DeepNorm (benefits "diminish" as weights drift). Under MuonH, DeepNorm with β-scaled init should behave closer to KEEL than it does under AdamW. This prediction comes from the derivation alone; I found no paper that tests it.
- **Gain init in AttnRes and HC.** Zero query (uniform average) makes AttnRes ≡ Pre-LN at init. HC's static init (B = 1, A_m = e_(k mod n), A_r = I) also makes it ≡ Pre-LN. Both designs start inside the Pre-LN function and differ only through learned routing. That is why "freeze routing at init" ≈ Pre-LN, and why the static share of the routing (Q2) is the right quantity to measure.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that studies Pre-LN/Post-LN/LNS/DeepNorm equivalences under Muon or under norm-fixed (hyperspherical) optimizers. The derivations above are mine and unverified by experiment.
- I did not open DeepNet itself to quote its Adam-specific update bound. The Su post paraphrases it with SGD.

## Q5. How each source maps to the queued runs, and concrete next experiments

### Takeaway
The literature supports three predictions. (1) Static HC keeps most of HC's gain. (2) Static Full AttnRes keeps about half and loses its huge-norm "bias sources". (3) Pre-LN with 1/√ℓ gains matches LNS only if deep-layer gains stay small. Four cheap experiments follow directly from the literature: a null-route softmax, signed static weights, a learned HC readout mixer, and a block-layout fix.

### Cited Findings (the anchors for the predictions)
- HC static vs dynamic at n = 4: 55–67% of the gain kept (my arithmetic from HC Table 2). — [HC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3)
- AttnRes fully static mixing: 59% kept; DenseFormer 0%. — [AttnRes Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)
- MUDD static: 41–48% kept; DCA static per-dimension: 96%. — [MUDDFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170); [DCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06785)
- Block AttnRes dead segment before the first saved block. — [DepthBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- A softmax with no null route goes with sinks and outliers in AttnRes. — [OASIS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17887)
- DenseFormer learns negative embedding weights in late layers. — [DenseFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)
- mHC's doubly stochastic constraint collapses the effective rank of transport (1.44–1.65 vs 2.53–2.81). — [DepthBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32534)
- H_res = I reportedly suffices for mHC (state expansion matters, mixing less). — [AttnRes §6.2 citing Xie](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15031)

### Inferences: predictions for the queued runs
1. **Pre-LN with gain init 1/√ℓ.** If deep-layer gains in the run stay within ~2× of 1/√ℓ, expect loss within noise of LNS. If they grow toward 1, expect it to fall back toward Pre-LN. Either result explains what LNS does under MuonH (see Q4).
2. **HC, static routing from scratch.** Expect ≈70–100% of HC's gain over Pre-LN. The anchors: SHC at n = 2 equals DHC; at n = 4, 55–67%; the project's freeze test 78–95%; short training limits how far dynamic terms grow. Static HC keeps the layer-1 signed mixing, which the project found carries most of the value. If static HC instead loses most of the gain, then the dynamic part does something the token mean cannot, such as stabilization or per-token sign flips. The next check would be the per-token variance of the layer-1 mixing.
3. **Full AttnRes, static routing from scratch.** Expect ≈30–60% of the gain (anchor 59% at 16 layers; lower at 48 layers because selection among 97 sources matters more). Also expect no 10⁴×-type source and lower logit gaps. If static AttnRes keeps ≥80%, then the dynamic key dependence matters little and AttnRes is mostly a learned static DenseNet with softmax positivity.

### Inferences: next experiments suggested by the literature
- **A. Null-route depth softmax (Softmax₁) in AttnRes** (OASIS-style, depth level only). Prediction: the 53,000× source and the 32–67-nat gaps shrink, because "ignore all sources" no longer needs one sacrificial source or extreme logits. Loss changes by ≤ noise. Cost: one scalar per reader.
- **B. Signed static weights.** Compare a static AttnRes with softmax weights against one with unconstrained real weights, both initialized at the uniform/Pre-LN point. DenseFormer's negative embedding weights and HC's −0.81 suggest that subtraction is used. AttnRes's non-negativity (a design choice per Su) may cost something at depth.
- **C. A learned HC readout mixer** (V4-style, per DepthBench) in place of the stream sum. Prediction: logit-lens alignment of early HC layers improves under the mixer's readout, which resolves the "reaches head coordinates by 3/4 depth" vs DepthBench tension. Final loss changes little.
- **D. A Block AttnRes layout without a dead segment** (e.g. save the first block after 1–2 layers, or let the first block read its own partial sum with a positive bias). DepthBench's account predicts that the gain from depth returns at deep shapes. The project's near-binary block-1 reads are the same symptom.
- **E. mHC with H_res = I** (state expansion only), for comparison with HC and mHC. This tests whether the rank collapse that DepthBench blames on doubly stochastic mixing explains why mHC "behaves like the others" in the project.

### Gaps
- No published work trains per-layer "separate heads" (deep supervision) on top of HC or AttnRes. I found nothing on why HC pays half the last-layer cost of per-layer losses. This is open.
- No paper measures K3's internal routing. The project's K3 analyses appear to be first-hand.
